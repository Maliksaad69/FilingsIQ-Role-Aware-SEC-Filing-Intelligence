# AI_NOTES.md

AI tools (specifically LLMs for query rewriting, prompt experimentation, and evaluation judging) were leveraged to accelerate pipeline development, generate financial domain synonyms, and grade retrieved evidence against gold answers. However, during Experiment 3 (evaluating legal proceedings disclosures in PepsiCo's 10-K), the generation model was badly steered by boilerplate legal language: when reading management's statement that "the final outcome of these matters will not have a material adverse effect on the company's consolidated financial position," the model incorrectly inferred that no individual proceeding was materially important, asserting that PepsiCo had no material legal battles. I noticed this failure because the SEC filing explicitly disclosed ongoing legal proceedings without assessing the independent materiality of each individual action—it only concluded that aggregate financial impact was bounded. Relying on the LLM's initial interpretation would have produced a legally false assertion for research analysts. To rectify this, I re-engineered the system prompt with strict interpretation boundaries: forcing the LLM to separate (a) whether proceedings exist, (b) individual matter materiality, and (c) management's aggregate expected financial effect, while requiring an explicit disclaimer ("the filing does not establish whether any individual proceeding is independently material") whenever individual materiality is undisclosed.
